# Dedicated transfer history

Transfers is a pushed page at `/transfers`, available from Accounts, the Transactions transfer strip, Home's "N transfers need an answer" notice and activity links on Home/Spending. It is the only transfers screen: the old bulk review address `/review-transfers` redirects here, and a link to one entry (`/review-transfers?transactionId=…` or `/transfers?transactionId=…`) opens that entry's *What is this?* sheet on this page (a missing entry or a row that is not a transfer opens nothing and says so in a dismissible line). It follows the selected reporting period (the period pill sits in the navigation row, the Spending pattern) and has search.

## Browsing and accounting

- **One question.** Whenever Wafra cannot tell, it asks *What is this?* with three answers. For an outgoing row: **Moved to my own account**, **Paid someone else**, **Not a transfer**. For an incoming row: **Came from my own account**, **Received from someone**, **Not a transfer** (Arabic equivalents are in `transfer-activity-copy.ts`). Each answer states its effect: own is not counted as spending or income, someone else is counted, and Not a transfer is a normal purchase, bill or income.
- **Three answers, three stored decisions.** The answers are the ownership values `'own'`, `'external'` and `'none'`. `'none'` is a reversible decision that makes the row an ordinary transaction: it leaves Transfers, no longer needs an answer, and counts in totals and Net like any purchase or income. Undo removes the decision.
- **Summary tiles.** Under the headline (the total moved between your accounts, not counted as spending or income) three tiles show **Your accounts**, **Sent to others** and **Received**. Sent and Received are counted in totals. Tapping a tile filters the list to that kind of row and tapping it again clears the filter; an empty filtered view offers a clear action. A linked own-account pair counts once. At large text sizes the tiles move from the band into the list header so they scroll away instead of crowding the list out.
- **Needs an answer: one term, one count.** Every transfer whose ownership is not settled is kept out of Spending, Income and Net and reads **Needs an answer** (Arabic **بانتظار إجابتك**) wherever it is shown: the Transfers route rows and groups, the Transactions row and entry sheet (**Needs an answer · not counted yet**), the Transactions summary and Home's Needs you pill (**N transfers need an answer**). "Not answered yet", "Transfer needs review · not counted yet", "kept apart" and "N transfers to answer" are gone. The count is `transferWaitingSummary` (`transfer-people.ts`): every unconfirmed transfer Transfers lists, a linked or likely own-account pair once. Home's pill, the Transfers card's title and (all time, no filters) the Transactions summary give the same number; the Transactions summary counts the same rule within its filters. Before 9 October 2026 only the reconciler's queue (`pendingIds`, a credible own-account match) was counted and asked about, so a ledger with 2,700 transfers to and from named people said "117 need an answer" while keeping all of them out of the totals.
- **People (Transfers grouped by person).** Every waiting transfer the reconciler does not queue (ownership unknown, no likely own-account pair, no ambiguous candidates, no card repayment, no decision) and whose bank alert names the other side (`transferEvidence.counterpartyName`, validated as the reconciler validates it; never a title) belongs to that person. Names are grouped by their letters only (`transferPersonKey`: Unicode NFKC, case, spacing, dots, hyphens and apostrophes, Arabic short vowels and tatweel); titles, accents, alef forms and initials are never folded, because two spellings left apart cost one extra question while two people merged would let one answer decide for both. A person holds money both ways: one row per person under **Waiting for your answer** with their initial, the name as the bank wrote it most often, **N transfers**, **Sent AED … (n)** and **Received AED … (n)**, the date range and **Needs an answer**, most transfers first. The first five are listed, then **Show all N people**. Rows with own-account evidence keep their own question and never join a person, so a person's answer is never mixed with a suggestion that one of their transfers moved between the person's accounts. A transfer that names nobody is not grouped: it stays its own route row and its own question. A person's entries are not listed a second time on their own; search narrows a person to the entries it matches (the count, totals and dates then describe exactly those); a tile filter hides the people.
- **A person is answered whole.** A person is one specific other side, so one answer covers every transfer with them: **your own account** (this name is you or an account of yours: not counted), **someone else** (counted as spending and income) or **not a transfer** (ordinary purchase or income). The answers are worded for money out, money in, or both (**Between my own accounts / With someone else / Not transfers**). Tapping a person, or answering the card's question about one, opens the confirmation sheet with the person, a line saying the answer covers every waiting transfer with them, the answer (preselected from the card), every entry (20 at a time) and **Save answer for N entries**; nothing is written before Save. The save is the same `resolveTransferBatch` request as a group's: one single-entry decision per transfer (own is never a pair link and never a legacy group decision), fingerprints and generation frozen when the person was picked, a changed transfer blocking Save, the app-wide transfer write lock, the durability retry and the toast's **Undo**. Wafra does not remember a person's answer for their next transfer: there is no counterparty rule today.
- **Question card.** One question at a time, whatever the period or search: first each person, most transfers first ("**Mariam S: your own account or someone else?**", with how many transfers, the money each way and the dates), then the reconciler's queue of unanswered rows (its `pendingIds`), newest first, then every other waiting transfer that names nobody, newest first (`transferQuestions`). Its title is the number of transfers waiting; "n of N" counts questions. A person's answer opens the confirmation sheet described above. For an entry, the card shows it with its three answers as one-tap buttons, "n of N", a hint when the evidence points at one of your accounts, and **See all N**, which turns on select mode on this page. Several possible accounts for an own answer open the detail sheet on its account picker. Saving an answer takes the row out of the queue, shows a **Saved · answer** toast with **Undo**, and the card moves to the next row. A linked or likely own-account pair whose two legs are both queued is one movement: the card asks once, about its outgoing leg, and "n of N" and **See all N** count movements, not bank entries. Answering that leg takes both legs out of the queue (an own answer links them; after another answer the other leg still **Needs an answer** and is asked about after the queue).
- **Route rows.** Each transfer is one row reading **From → To** with the amount, the date and a tag: **Your accounts**, **Someone else · counted** or **Needs an answer**. A confirmed own-account pair is a single row built from its outgoing leg and is always listed whole: both legs come in with it, it belongs to the period of the outgoing leg, and search matches it when either leg matches. A row with a named recipient shows the name; an unknown end is a dashed **?**.
- **Groups.** Above the period's rows, under **Waiting for your answer**, the reconciler's pending groups (the same account, the same kind and the same other side) are one route row each with an **N entries** count, the newest and oldest dates and **Needs an answer**, whatever the period. A group is listed when it still holds several unanswered entries, or when one of its entries is listed nowhere else on the page (a likely card repayment, a row on an archived account), so every queued entry is reachable here. Legs of a suggested own-account pair are asked about as their own route and never join a group; a grouped entry is not listed a second time on its own. Search narrows a group to its matching entries; a tile filter hides the groups. A group of thousands of entries is still one row. Tapping it opens the same *What is this?* sheet with the route and its entries (20 at a time, **Show more**), each of which opens that entry's own sheet.
- **Answered whole, or one by one.** A group is answered as a whole only when the reconciler marked it bulk-eligible (one known other side), or when every entry is a suggestion (likely own-account or likely card repayment) whose matching entry is on the same one other account. Its sheet then shows the three answers, and nothing is saved until an answer is picked and **Save answer for N entries** is pressed. A catch-all group (ambiguous or ownership unknown, typically **Account → ?**) is never answered whole: its sheet only lists the entries, says each needs its own answer, and offers **Done**. Entries whose id is stored twice cannot be answered at all (the store refuses them): they are split into their own read-only row that says why, which is never pressed, ticked or saved.
- **Looks like, and Linked to.** When the reconciler suggests where an entry's money went (a likely own-account pair, or a likely card repayment: a payment received on one of the person's credit cards with the same amount, minutes apart), the sheet shows **Looks like your ADCB Credit Card ••7720** under *Moved to my own account*, with the matching entry itself (what it was, its date and time, its amount) and "Same amount, close in time: a suggestion, not confirmed until you answer.", and the group row says the same. **Linked to** is said only of a confirmed link. When the entries answered together look like different accounts, the line under the own answer says each entry shows its own, and each listed entry (or ticked item in the confirmation) names its own matching account; names are never merged into one line. In a group's sheet each listed entry names its own matching entry the same way. This carries the old review sheet's "Matching bank entry" and "suggestion, not confirmed" information into the one sheet; it is the same in an entry's own detail sheet, wherever that is opened from. A card repayment answered own is saved as an own decision without a pair link on every path (group, selection, the question card and the entry's own sheet, which offers no card receipt to link), as the review screen saved it; a suggested own-account pair answered own links both legs.
- **Select several.** **Select** in the list's heading turns on select mode: the list shows everything waiting for an answer, whatever the period (every person, then the groups, then each waiting entry, a suggested pair as one route), each row a checkbox; a person is one tick, answered whole; **Select all (N)**, **Clear** and **Cancel** sit above it, and a bar at the bottom shows how many are ticked and the same three answers (worded for both directions when money in and money out are ticked together, or when a suggested pair, which moves money out of one account and into another, is ticked). An answer opens the confirmation sheet with that answer chosen and every ticked item drawn as its route. A catch-all group is not a checkbox: it is a disclosure (**Show entries to tick one by one**) that lists its entries as rows of their own beneath it, each ticked on its own by hand; hiding them again unticks them. **Select all** never ticks a catch-all group whole or its opened entries, and a duplicate-id row never at all. Only what the list shows can be ticked: anything ticked that search, a closed group or the ledger takes off the list (a group, a lone entry or a pair) is unticked and stays unticked when it comes back. A ticked group answers exactly the entries its row showed when it was ticked: if its entries change, or it stops being answerable whole, it is unticked; a catch-all group that becomes whole drops the entries ticked on their own, so no entry is ever answered twice (the sheet also refuses picks that name one entry twice, and the resolver's refusal of a doubled entry reads as "These transfers changed").
- **Not listed as transfers.** Rows on archived accounts, which stay hidden as they are in every total, and rows whose id is shared by more than one stored record. The reconciler marks such duplicate-id observations ambiguous and still queues them; they stay in the regular transaction list and, while queued, are shown on this page read-only in their own row, with the reason they cannot be answered here. When nothing else waits, there is no "N need an answer · See all" link and no **Select**, since select mode would have nothing to tick.
- **Transactions strip.** The Transactions summary is stacked: the count (with period, filters and what is left out of the total) on one line, the net with its clear-filters action on the next, so neither is squeezed. A compact strip reads **n transfers listed in Transfers**, then which amounts sent to or received from others are in the net, then how many **need an answer** (every transfer in view still waiting, counted by the same rule as Home and the Transfers card, including one that stays in this list because its account is not identified). Both counts treat a linked or likely own-account pair as one transfer when both legs are separated and in view (one leg in view counts as one); the amounts are the same sums of every leg as before. The whole strip opens Transfers. Date, account, category, amount and search restrictions are applied before this explanation is calculated, and the filter-sheet previews use the same projection.
- **Detail sheets.** Every detail sheet for a transfer candidate asks the same question with the same answers (radio rows). Transactions and Transfers pass the assessment from the reconciliation they already hold; every other sheet (account, Flow, Home, card, merchant, currency, accuracy, assistant evidence) reads the reconciliation already cached for the stored final transfer receipt (`cachedTransferReconciliationForState`) while an entry is open. Opening an entry never builds the transfer graph; Home builds it after interactions on focus, and Transactions and Transfers build it for their lists, so the cache is normally warm. With that assessment a confirmed pair shows as answered with a **Linked to** line, and an own answer links a single likely counterpart or opens the account picker when several accounts could be the other side. When there is no cached result (a history import holding only a provisional receipt, a restored ledger with no receipt, or before any of those screens has run since the receipt changed), these sheets fall back to the row's own answer, with no link line and no picker. (Transfers always passes its own assessment; Transactions passes one whenever it has a reconciliation, which excludes a provisional import receipt.) Tapping the answer that is already selected saves nothing. Marking and unmarking an own transfer remain available only on rows the system does not itself flag.
- Card repayments (including likely ones), salary, cashback, refunds and corroborating duplicate observations are not swept into this view merely because they have an internal/legacy transfer flag.
- External transfers can legitimately contribute to income or spending, which is why the Transactions net includes them and the strip says so.
- Browsing does not edit the ledger. Answers and Undo retain the existing fingerprint, confirmation and durability checks.
- **Several at once is the bulk review's save, moved.** A group or a selection is saved with one `resolveTransferBatch` request of explicit single-entry decisions (a suggested pair answered own is one linked decision; answered otherwise, each leg is answered alone). It never sends a legacy multi-id group decision. The fingerprints of every row shown and the ledger generation are frozen when the item is picked; a changed row or a restore blocks Save and says so, a restore between ticking and answering refuses to open the sheet, a repeated tap writes once, and a failed encrypted write keeps the sheet (and the page) open on **Retry saving**, which only re-persists after checking the post-decision fingerprints against the current ledger; while it is pending, an entry cannot be opened from the sheet. The store refusing a moved ledger is reported as "These transfers changed", not as a failed save. The saved toast has **Undo**: the rows' fingerprints are taken as the save left them (the store's own post-decision fingerprints after a retried write; otherwise the ledger's rows, accepted only when every one carries exactly the decision this save wrote, stamped once and not before the save began, else Undo refuses), and Undo clears each changed row only if it is still exactly that; if an entry was answered again or edited since, nothing is cleared and the toast says it changed. An entry's own sheet offers **Clear my answer** for a saved answer, with the same check against the rows as shown; it clears the other side of a link only while that side's decision still names this entry, so a newer answer given to it is kept. Transfer writes go one at a time app-wide (one shared lock taken by every answer, group or selection save, Undo and Clear): an Undo pressed while a save is still writing is refused, and the other way round. While any answer, Undo or Clear on this page is being written (including the toast's Undo and the entry sheet opened over the page, which share the page's answer hook), the page's controls wait.

## Implementation boundaries

`transfer-activity.ts` uses the current reconciler's assessments for dedicated history membership. It does not introduce pairing rules or infer ownership. Each record carries `confirmed` (ownership settled) and, separately, `needsReview` (an unconfirmed row in the reconciler's `pendingIds`). Detail sheets receive the current assessment for consistent presentation. Copy lives in `transfer-activity-copy.ts` with matching English and Arabic keys.

`transfer-people.ts` builds the people from the same records, once per activity list (so once per stored transfer receipt), in one pass with no pairwise comparison: `transferPeople`, `transferWaitingSummary` (the one count) and `transferQuestions` (the card's order). `transfer-person-row.tsx` draws a person (row, avatar, money line, Show all toggle); the card and the confirmation sheet reuse its avatar and money line. A person enters the batch as a `TransferBatchItem` of kind `group` whose preview carries `person`. Fingerprints of the rows shown are cached per immutable row object in `transfer-batch.ts`, so an open sheet re-checking hundreds of rows on each render does not hash them again; the store's resolver still checks every fingerprint itself before it writes.

`transfer-list.ts` turns those records into the page's rows and totals: one `TransferListItem` per transfer (a confirmed own-account pair collapses to one From → To row, keyed `<outId>|<inId>`), the tile totals and the tile filter. `transfer-answer.ts` holds the one answer: the three `TransferAnswer` values, `currentAnswer`, the candidates for the other side of an own answer, and the request builders for saving and undoing an answer. They freeze the fingerprints the person saw, and Undo of a linked own answer clears both rows as two single-row decisions. `useTransferAnswer` runs those requests through the existing resolver and shows the "Saved" toast with Undo, so the Transfers page, the question card and every detail sheet answer in the same way with the same durability and stale-fingerprint checks. `transfer-batch.ts` builds the several-at-once request (`transferBatchDecisions`), the rows it changes, the frozen fingerprints and the groups the page lists (`transferAnswerGroups`); `useTransferBatch` holds the confirmation sheet's state and save, ported from the retired review screen with its guarantees unchanged.

The Transactions and Transfers screens reuse one reconciliation per stored transfer receipt (`transferReconciliationForState`). While a history import holds only a provisional receipt, Transactions separates nothing and Transfers builds the graph itself, so separation cannot disagree with the provisional internal set. Transactions separates exactly the rows `getTransferActivity` lists, and a row that neither counts in totals nor belongs to a live account keeps its hidden treatment even if named.

Home and Spending previews do not rebuild the transfer graph. They leave out only rows that the Transfers screen is guaranteed to list, using the row-local `isListedExternalTransfer` test: an explicitly external transfer with a unique id. Unknown-ownership rows, including likely card repayments that Transfers leaves to their own review, stay in recent activity rather than disappear. The existing live-account and internal-id rules apply first, and preview limits count only the rows that remain. The cash-flow predicate accepts read-only sets; its accounting logic is unchanged.
