# Dedicated transfer history

Transfers is a pushed page at `/transfers`, available from Accounts, the Transactions transfer strip, Home's "N transfers need an answer" notice and activity links on Home/Spending. It is the only transfers screen: the old bulk review address `/review-transfers` redirects here, and a link to one entry (`/review-transfers?transactionId=…` or `/transfers?transactionId=…`) opens that entry's *What is this?* sheet on this page (a missing entry or a row that is not a transfer opens nothing and says so in a dismissible line). It follows the selected reporting period (the period pill sits in the navigation row, the Spending pattern) and has search.

## Browsing and accounting

- **One question.** Whenever Wafra cannot tell, it asks *What is this?* with three answers. For an outgoing row: **Moved to my own account**, **Paid someone else**, **Not a transfer**. For an incoming row: **Came from my own account**, **Received from someone**, **Not a transfer** (Arabic equivalents are in `transfer-activity-copy.ts`). Each answer states its effect: own is not counted as spending or income, someone else is counted, and Not a transfer is a normal purchase, bill or income.
- **Three answers, three stored decisions.** The answers are the ownership values `'own'`, `'external'` and `'none'`. `'none'` is a reversible decision that makes the row an ordinary transaction: it leaves Transfers, no longer needs an answer, and counts in totals and Net like any purchase or income. Undo removes the decision.
- **Summary tiles.** Under the headline (the total moved between your accounts, not counted as spending or income) three tiles show **Your accounts**, **Sent to others** and **Received**. Sent and Received are counted in totals. Tapping a tile filters the list to that kind of row and tapping it again clears the filter; an empty filtered view offers a clear action. A linked own-account pair counts once. At large text sizes the tiles move from the band into the list header so they scroll away instead of crowding the list out.
- **Question card.** The reconciler's queue of unanswered rows (its `pendingIds`), newest first, whatever the period or search. The card shows the first one with its three answers as one-tap buttons, "n of N", a hint when the evidence points at one of your accounts, and **See all N**, which turns on select mode on this page. Several possible accounts for an own answer open the detail sheet on its account picker. Saving an answer takes the row out of the queue, shows a **Saved · answer** toast with **Undo**, and the card moves to the next row. A linked or likely own-account pair whose two legs are both queued is one movement: the card asks once, about its outgoing leg, and "n of N" and **See all N** count movements, not bank entries. Answering that leg takes both legs out of the queue (an own answer links them; after another answer the other leg stays **Not answered yet** without a question of its own).
- **Route rows.** Each transfer is one row reading **From → To** with the amount, the date and a tag: **Your accounts**, **Someone else · counted**, **Needs an answer** or **Not answered yet**. A confirmed own-account pair is a single row built from its outgoing leg and is always listed whole: both legs come in with it, it belongs to the period of the outgoing leg, and search matches it when either leg matches. A row with a named recipient shows the name; an unknown end is a dashed **?**.
- **Groups.** Above the period's rows, under **Waiting for your answer**, the reconciler's pending groups (the same account, the same kind and the same other side) are one route row each with an **N entries** count, the newest and oldest dates and **Needs an answer**, whatever the period. A group is listed when it still holds several unanswered entries, or when one of its entries is listed nowhere else on the page (a likely card repayment, a row on an archived account), so every queued entry is reachable here. Legs of a suggested own-account pair are asked about as their own route and never join a group; a grouped entry is not listed a second time on its own. Search narrows a group to its matching entries; a tile filter hides the groups. A group of thousands of entries is still one row. Tapping it opens the same *What is this?* sheet with the route and its entries (20 at a time, **Show more**), each of which opens that entry's own sheet.
- **Answered whole, or one by one.** A group is answered as a whole only when the reconciler marked it bulk-eligible (one known other side), or when every entry is a suggestion (likely own-account or likely card repayment) whose matching entry is on the same one other account. Its sheet then shows the three answers, and nothing is saved until an answer is picked and **Save answer for N entries** is pressed. A catch-all group (ambiguous or ownership unknown, typically **Account → ?**) is never answered whole: its sheet only lists the entries, says each needs its own answer, and offers **Done**. Entries whose id is stored twice cannot be answered at all (the store refuses them): they are split into their own read-only row that says why, which is never pressed, ticked or saved.
- **Looks like, and Linked to.** When the reconciler suggests where an entry's money went (a likely own-account pair, or a likely card repayment: a payment received on one of the person's credit cards with the same amount, minutes apart), the sheet shows **Looks like your ADCB Credit Card ••7720** under *Moved to my own account*, with the matching entry itself (what it was, its date and time, its amount) and "Same amount, close in time: a suggestion, not confirmed until you answer.", and the group row says the same. **Linked to** is said only of a confirmed link. When the entries answered together look like different accounts, the line under the own answer says each entry shows its own, and each listed entry (or ticked item in the confirmation) names its own matching account; names are never merged into one line. In a group's sheet each listed entry names its own matching entry the same way. This carries the old review sheet's "Matching bank entry" and "suggestion, not confirmed" information into the one sheet; it is the same in an entry's own detail sheet, wherever that is opened from. A card repayment answered own is saved as an own decision without a pair link on every path (group, selection, the question card and the entry's own sheet, which offers no card receipt to link), as the review screen saved it; a suggested own-account pair answered own links both legs.
- **Select several.** **Select** in the list's heading turns on select mode: the list shows everything waiting for an answer, whatever the period (groups first, then each queued entry, a suggested pair as one route), each row a checkbox; **Select all (N)**, **Clear** and **Cancel** sit above it, and a bar at the bottom shows how many are ticked and the same three answers (worded for both directions when money in and money out are ticked together, or when a suggested pair, which moves money out of one account and into another, is ticked). An answer opens the confirmation sheet with that answer chosen and every ticked item drawn as its route. A catch-all group is not a checkbox: it is a disclosure (**Show entries to tick one by one**) that lists its entries as rows of their own beneath it, each ticked on its own; hiding them again unticks them. **Select all** never ticks a catch-all group whole, and a duplicate-id row never at all. A ticked group answers exactly the entries its row showed when it was ticked: if search (or the ledger) changes the entries the row shows, it is unticked.
- **Not listed as transfers.** Rows on archived accounts, which stay hidden as they are in every total, and rows whose id is shared by more than one stored record. The reconciler marks such duplicate-id observations ambiguous and still queues them; they stay in the regular transaction list and, while queued, are shown on this page read-only in their own row, with the reason they cannot be answered here.
- **Transactions strip.** The Transactions summary is stacked: the count (with period, filters and what is left out of the total) on one line, the net with its clear-filters action on the next, so neither is squeezed. A compact strip reads **n transfers kept apart**, then which amounts sent to or received from others are in the net, then how many need an answer. Both counts treat a linked or likely own-account pair as one transfer when both legs are separated and in view (one leg in view counts as one); the amounts are the same sums of every leg as before. The whole strip opens Transfers. Date, account, category, amount and search restrictions are applied before this explanation is calculated, and the filter-sheet previews use the same projection.
- **Detail sheets.** Every detail sheet for a transfer candidate asks the same question with the same answers (radio rows). Transactions and Transfers pass the assessment from the reconciliation they already hold; every other sheet (account, Flow, Home, card, merchant, currency, accuracy, assistant evidence) reads the reconciliation already cached for the stored final transfer receipt (`cachedTransferReconciliationForState`) while an entry is open. Opening an entry never builds the transfer graph; Home builds it after interactions on focus, and Transactions and Transfers build it for their lists, so the cache is normally warm. With that assessment a confirmed pair shows as answered with a **Linked to** line, and an own answer links a single likely counterpart or opens the account picker when several accounts could be the other side. When there is no cached result (a history import holding only a provisional receipt, a restored ledger with no receipt, or before any of those screens has run since the receipt changed), these sheets fall back to the row's own answer, with no link line and no picker. (Transfers always passes its own assessment; Transactions passes one whenever it has a reconciliation, which excludes a provisional import receipt.) Tapping the answer that is already selected saves nothing. Marking and unmarking an own transfer remain available only on rows the system does not itself flag.
- Card repayments (including likely ones), salary, cashback, refunds and corroborating duplicate observations are not swept into this view merely because they have an internal/legacy transfer flag.
- External transfers can legitimately contribute to income or spending, which is why the Transactions net includes them and the strip says so.
- Browsing does not edit the ledger. Answers and Undo retain the existing fingerprint, confirmation and durability checks.
- **Several at once is the bulk review's save, moved.** A group or a selection is saved with one `resolveTransferBatch` request of explicit single-entry decisions (a suggested pair answered own is one linked decision; answered otherwise, each leg is answered alone). It never sends a legacy multi-id group decision. The fingerprints of every row shown and the ledger generation are frozen when the item is picked; a changed row or a restore blocks Save and says so, a restore between ticking and answering refuses to open the sheet, a repeated tap writes once, and a failed encrypted write keeps the sheet (and the page) open on **Retry saving**, which only re-persists after checking the post-decision fingerprints against the current ledger; while it is pending, an entry cannot be opened from the sheet. The store refusing a moved ledger is reported as "These transfers changed", not as a failed save. The saved toast has **Undo**: the rows' fingerprints are taken as the save left them, and Undo clears each changed row only if it is still exactly that; if an entry was answered again or edited since, nothing is cleared and the toast says it changed. An entry's own sheet offers **Clear my answer** for a saved answer, with the same check against the rows as shown; it clears the other side of a link only while that side's decision still names this entry, so a newer answer given to it is kept. While any answer, Undo or Clear on this page is being written (including the toast's Undo and the entry sheet opened over the page, which share the page's answer hook), the page's controls wait.

## Implementation boundaries

`transfer-activity.ts` uses the current reconciler's assessments for dedicated history membership. It does not introduce pairing rules or infer ownership. Each record carries `confirmed` (ownership settled) and, separately, `needsReview` (an unconfirmed row in the reconciler's `pendingIds`). Detail sheets receive the current assessment for consistent presentation. Copy lives in `transfer-activity-copy.ts` with matching English and Arabic keys.

`transfer-list.ts` turns those records into the page's rows and totals: one `TransferListItem` per transfer (a confirmed own-account pair collapses to one From → To row, keyed `<outId>|<inId>`), the tile totals and the tile filter. `transfer-answer.ts` holds the one answer: the three `TransferAnswer` values, `currentAnswer`, the candidates for the other side of an own answer, and the request builders for saving and undoing an answer. They freeze the fingerprints the person saw, and Undo of a linked own answer clears both rows as two single-row decisions. `useTransferAnswer` runs those requests through the existing resolver and shows the "Saved" toast with Undo, so the Transfers page, the question card and every detail sheet answer in the same way with the same durability and stale-fingerprint checks. `transfer-batch.ts` builds the several-at-once request (`transferBatchDecisions`), the rows it changes, the frozen fingerprints and the groups the page lists (`transferAnswerGroups`); `useTransferBatch` holds the confirmation sheet's state and save, ported from the retired review screen with its guarantees unchanged.

The Transactions and Transfers screens reuse one reconciliation per stored transfer receipt (`transferReconciliationForState`). While a history import holds only a provisional receipt, Transactions separates nothing and Transfers builds the graph itself, so separation cannot disagree with the provisional internal set. Transactions separates exactly the rows `getTransferActivity` lists, and a row that neither counts in totals nor belongs to a live account keeps its hidden treatment even if named.

Home and Spending previews do not rebuild the transfer graph. They leave out only rows that the Transfers screen is guaranteed to list, using the row-local `isListedExternalTransfer` test: an explicitly external transfer with a unique id. Unknown-ownership rows, including likely card repayments that Transfers leaves to their own review, stay in recent activity rather than disappear. The existing live-account and internal-id rules apply first, and preview limits count only the rows that remain. The cash-flow predicate accepts read-only sets; its accounting logic is unchanged.
